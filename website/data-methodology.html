Methodology

How we build holdings data you can defend in front of a client.

Fund-holdings analytics is only as good as its pipeline. This page documents the exact mechanics — sources, priority order, matching logic, confidence scoring, refresh cadence and gap disclosure — so you can inspect every claim before you rely on it.

Step 1 — Acquisition

Three public sources, one fixed priority order

Every scheme's holdings are produced from up to three sources and merged identically for all schemes. Within the same source, the snapshot with the best %-of-NAV coverage wins.

SourceWhat it providesWhy it ranks here
1AMFI monthly portfolio disclosure The industry-standardised monthly portfolio of every scheme: security, ISIN, sector, rating, market value and % to NAV for every holding, published within ten working days of month-end. Authoritative, complete, machine-readable — the primary outcome in one place.
2Individual AMC websites Each asset manager's own monthly portfolio document, parsed into the same normalised shape (scheme, date, weighted holdings). Official and granular, but fragmented across 50+ different formats and locations.
3Third-party republisher Scheme-level holdings with names and ISINs; weights often need to be computed from values. Used only to fill schemes that have neither official source yet.
✓Benchmark resolution (index & ETF) Official index constituent lists with constituent weights. Index/ETF schemes hold their benchmark, not a discretionary book — this resolves them correctly instead of counting them as missing.
AMFI→ AMC website→ Republisher→ Benchmark (index/ETF)

If a scheme's data ever traces to the republisher, the interface labels it — you're never left guessing which tier produced a number.

Step 2 — Normalisation & matching

Making thousands of disclosures comparable

Coverage claims mean nothing until "every scheme" is defined rigorously. Ours is matched against the industry's combined NAV universe — 2,691 funds / 3,812 fund-and-plan rows as of the August 2026 universe file.

Direct ≡ Regular, matched at fund level

Direct and Regular plans hold the same underlying portfolio, so schemes are compared and counted at fund level. This prevents double-counting plans and keeps coverage statistics honest.

Index & ETF resolution

Index-based schemes are matched to their tracked benchmark's constituent list. Resolved equity index schemes inherit full constituent holdings with ISINs; genuinely unresolvable cases (e.g., certain debt or overseas indices without public weight files) are explicitly categorised.

Identity cleaning

Holding rows pass ISIN validation and company-name normalisation. A dedicated reference database of unique equity ISINs backs enrichment with sector and market-cap buckets.

ISIN completeness drive

Missing-ISIN holdings were cut by ~94% through re-parses from cleaner monthly workbooks and systematic lookups against index constituents — because overlap math is only correct when both sides identify securities the same way.

Step 3 — Scoring

A confidence badge on every scheme

Not all holdings data is equally trustworthy, and pretending otherwise is how bad decisions happen. Each scheme carries a 0–100 transparency score built from three signals:

① Source quality (base score)

Official AMFI disclosure scores highest; own-AMC disclosures next; benchmark-resolved index portfolios follow; third-party republication scores lowest.

② Disclosure recency (age penalty)

Fresh disclosures lose nothing; staleness beyond roughly five weeks progressively reduces the score, heavily penalising anything older than six months.

③ Holdings quality (blend)

The score is blended with row-level quality: what fraction of the scheme's holdings carry valid ISINs and clean %NAV weights.

Tiers you'll see

High ≥ 80  Medium ≥ 55  Low < 55  No-data

Hovering a badge shows exactly what drove it: source, disclosure age, ISIN coverage. No black boxes.

Step 5 — Boundaries

Honest gap disclosure

Around 98% of active fund-level schemes carry holdings today. The remainder are catalogued, not hidden:

"No disclosure" is a label, not a void

Schemes whose managers publish nothing usable are flagged as such and excluded from holdings views — never silently dropped or padded with estimates.

An explicit backlog

Unresolved schemes are maintained in a categorized backlog (no-disclosure managers, debt-index schemes needing external weight files, overseas benchmarks) and worked down month by month.

FAQ

Methodology questions, answered plainly


What is the source priority order for holdings data?
Holdings are merged in one fixed priority order applied identically to every scheme: AMFI's standardised monthly portfolio disclosure ranks first because it publishes percentage-to-NAV weights for every holding of every scheme in one machine-readable place; individual AMC website monthly portfolios rank second; a third-party republisher ranks last and only fills schemes that have neither of the official disclosures.
How are index funds and ETFs handled?
Index and ETF schemes hold their benchmark rather than an actively disclosed portfolio, so they are resolved against official index constituent lists instead of AMC documents. Equity index schemes map to their index constituents with full ISIN lists; schemes whose benchmarks cannot be locally resolved are explicitly flagged as unresolved rather than silently omitted.
What does a confidence badge measure?
Each scheme carries a 0-100 data-confidence score that starts from its source quality (official AMFI disclosure scores highest, third-party republication lowest), subtracts a penalty as the disclosure ages beyond about five weeks, and blends in holdings quality measured by ISIN coverage and weight coverage of its holding rows. Scores map to tiers: High (green) at 80 or above, Medium (amber) at 55 or above, Low (red) below that, and Grey when no disclosure exists at all.
How often is the data refreshed?
Portfolio holdings follow the industry's monthly disclosure cycle and are re-fetched at the start of every month. NAV history, stock prices, corporate actions and the NSE bond catalogue are refreshed daily through scheduled automated pipelines. Every figure in the workspace displays its own as-of date.
What happens when a scheme has no disclosure?
Schemes without any obtainable disclosure are labelled 'no disclosure' and excluded from holdings views rather than being silently dropped or filled with estimates. The platform maintains an explicit backlog of uncovered schemes so users can see exactly where the boundaries of the data are.
