The problem

Why fund-holdings data in India is genuinely hard

Every asset manager publishes its own way. Before FundPulse, using holdings data meant manual work no adviser has time for.

Fragmented disclosuresEach of 50+ AMCs publishes monthly portfolios in different formats, layouts and locations — Excel, PDF factsheets, third-party sites.
Inconsistent identifiersMissing or malformed ISINs, mangled company names and sheet codes make clean matching difficult without heavy cleaning.
Index & ETF blind spotsIndex funds hold the benchmark, not a disclosure — they need resolution against index constituent lists to be counted at all.
No single truthThe same scheme appears differently across republishers; without a documented priority order, two tools give you two answers.
Our mechanism

Data-integrity rules we hold ourselves to

These aren't aspirations — they're the enforced mechanics of the pipeline. The full technical detail is public: read the methodology page.

1

Fixed source priority — the same for every scheme

AMFI's standardised monthly disclosure wins first, individual AMC disclosures second, third-party republication last. Index/ETF funds resolve to benchmark constituents. You always know which source produced a number — it's labelled on-screen.

2

A confidence badge on every scheme

Schemes carry High / Medium / Low / No-data badges computed from source quality, disclosure recency and ISIN coverage. If a number is weakly supported, you see it before you rely on it.

3

Documented refresh cadence

Holdings follow the monthly disclosure cycle; NAV history, stock prices, corporate actions and the bond catalogue refresh daily via scheduled pipelines. Every screen shows its as-of date.

4

Honest gap disclosure

Schemes without any disclosure are flagged as such rather than silently dropped. Where coverage ends, the tool says so — that honesty is the product.

5

Diagnostics, never advice

The engine produces verifiable facts about portfolio composition. It does not generate buy/sell calls, target prices or performance claims — those belong to the regulated professional using it.

FAQ

Frequently asked questions


What is FundPulse?
FundPulse is a web workspace that aggregates the monthly portfolio-holdings disclosures of every mutual-fund scheme from Indian AMCs — over 250,000 holding rows across 50+ AMCs as of August 2026 — into one searchable analytics workspace. It shows what each scheme actually holds, detects overlap between schemes, flags concentration risk, and generates client-ready proposals.
Who is FundPulse built for?
It is built for SEBI-registered Investment Advisers (RIAs), Research Analysts and mutual-fund distributors who need scheme-level holdings facts for portfolio construction, client reviews and compliance documentation.
Where does the holdings data come from?
Holdings come from up to three public sources, merged in a fixed priority order: AMFI's standardised monthly portfolio disclosure first, then individual AMC website disclosures, then a third-party republisher, with index and ETF funds resolved to their benchmark constituent lists. The same priority order is applied consistently to every scheme.
How current is the data?
Monthly portfolio holdings follow each AMC's monthly disclosure cycle. NAV history, stock prices, corporate actions and the NSE bond catalog are refreshed daily by automated pipelines. Every figure shown carries its own as-of date.
Does FundPulse give investment advice?
No. It is a diagnostic tool for factual analysis only and is not investment advice. It surfaces verifiable facts about scheme portfolios; decisions and recommendations remain entirely with the adviser and their clients.
What do confidence badges mean?
Every scheme carries a transparent data-confidence badge computed from its source quality (for example, official AMFI disclosure scores highest), how recently the disclosure was published, and holdings quality measures such as ISIN coverage. High (green), Medium (amber), Low (red) or No-data (grey) — so you always know how well-supported each number is.
